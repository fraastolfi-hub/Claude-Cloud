The Positioning Canvas

The six steps are the process. The canvas is where you pin it down: a single, clear page you can reread and hand to the people who work with you. The iron rule: you fill in the four boxes at the top. The fifth, the category, you don’t choose. You derive it.

1

Competitive alternatives

If you did not exist, what would the guest use instead? Include the status quo (staying at home, a rented apartment, a day spa in town) and the competitors that really make their shortlist: not every similar hotel, only the ones they seriously consider. Group them by approach.

2

Distinctive facts

What only you have: services, size, location, history, skills. Facts, not opinions. It is fine if they are not unique against every alternative: list them.

That is an opinion. Only verifiable facts go here.

3

Differentiating value

For each fact, without mercy: so what? Why should the guest care? The real value, not the feature. Group them into one to three themes.

It is the same ladder: fact → benefit → transformation.

4

Best-fit guests (and who you exclude)

The category is the consequence of everything above. If it does not hold up against the four boxes, you chose it by gut feeling.

5

Market category

Now reread the four boxes. The category is their logical consequence: the context that makes your value obvious, the point from which the guest understands who you are. Write it in one line: “the hotel of / for ___”.

That is not a category. It is the crowded box. Reread the four above and derive something nobody in your area holds.
