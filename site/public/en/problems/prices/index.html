N°01

Price doesn't reward the product. It rewards the reason.

A better product justifies a higher price only if the guest understands why.

The market pays for what it understands.

The nearby hotel that charges more usually has a clear reason. Or at least it has written one down.

You have the product. The reason, perhaps, you've never put into words.

To a guest, a premium without a reason is a surcharge.

01

Your average rate has a ceiling.

Above a certain figure, bookings slow down. The product would be worth more, but the guest can't see why.

To a guest, a premium without a reason is a surcharge.

02

The product grows, the price list doesn't.

Renovated rooms, a reworked breakfast, better reviews. The rate is the one from three years ago.

You invested in the product, not in the reason to pay for it.

03

Rates never touched.

You're afraid guests will stop coming. A rise mostly loses you the guests least suited to your hotel.

Low rates attract guests who choose on price.

04

The last-minute discount.

A guest asks for a discount close to the date and gets it: an empty room earns nothing.

Repeated, it teaches the market to wait.

05

Free upgrades in low season.

The room was empty anyway. But guests learn to wait for a deal, even in July.

A discount, repeated, becomes the price.

06

Your benchmark is the cheapest hotel.

You look at the hotels nearby and price yourself a little below. The comparison becomes the price.

On price, the chains win: they have costs and volumes an independent doesn't.

07

“Good value for money.”

It's the most frequent phrase in your reviews. It sounds like a compliment.

It means the deciding factor was price.

08

The seasonal seesaw.

Discounts in low season, full in high season. Then the cycle starts again.

You react to the season instead of building demand of your own.

09

The guests lost after a rate rise.

You raise the rate and the guests who only look at price go elsewhere. It feels like a loss.

Often it's a filter: the room goes to the guests who see its value.

10

Occupancy known, margin per room less so.

Full occupancy is reassuring. How much is left per room and per guest gets looked at less often.

The data is already there: it needs reading together.

Case · Silva Splendid · Fiuggi

From “4-star hotel with spa” to The Wellness Hotel of Fiuggi.

  1. 118 rooms and the largest spa in Lazio. Yet it spoke to everyone, and nobody remembered it.
  2. The choice: a fact instead of an adjective, a category with the definite article (in Italian, L’Hotel Benessere di Fiuggi), and almost no families any more.
  3. In five years, revenue goes from €5.6 million to €7 million. The average rate roughly doubles.

FAQ

The questions I'm asked most often.

The market in my area won't accept higher rates.

Often, in the same area, there's a similar hotel that charges more and stays full. The price that holds depends on the reason the guest recognises, more than on the rooms.

If I raise my rates, will I lose bookings?

With a clear reason, you mostly lose the ones least suited to your hotel. Without one, you simply lose them. That's why positioning comes before the price list.

Isn't a revenue manager enough?

A revenue manager works on the price the market accepts. Positioning changes the price the market considers fair. First the compass, then the fine-tuning.

Who puts it into practice?

Your team or your suppliers. I provide the direction, the copy and the scripts. The full analysis also covers how to handle the price objection.
