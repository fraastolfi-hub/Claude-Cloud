N°02

What I do. And what I don't.

I work on one thing: making your hotel recognisable, hard to mistake for any other.

I don't do

  • Advertising.
  • Logos.
  • “More visibility”. Without positioning, visibility brings guests who forget you quickly.
  • Revenue management, OTA management, campaigns.
  • Running the hotel for you. I give the direction and the words. Running it stays with you.

I do

  • I find the position your hotel already has and isn't using.
  • I decide with you who to leave out.
  • Then I write it down, so nobody mistakes you for someone else.
  • I put it on one sheet that keeps your website, front desk, campaigns and review replies aligned.
First you decide the position, then you write it. It's one single piece of work.

N°04

How I see it. The market, and AI.

Five convictions that guide my work.

  1. In the hotel market, properties look alike.

    Same photos, same promises, same adjectives. Here quality isn't enough: you need to be different. And the difference doesn't come from a refurbishment. It comes from a clear position.

  2. Booking doesn't steal your guests.

    It picks them up when the hotel doesn't say who it is. Then the list does the comparing: one price next to another price.

  3. Looking after the product isn't enough if someone else sets the price.

    My grandfather grew some of the best peaches in the province, but the price was set by a middleman who opened two crates at random. “Selling isn't my job,” he used to say. Many hotels look after their product the same way, and leave the price to others.

  4. An AI assistant only describes well what is already clear.

    Booking shows a list. An AI assistant proposes a choice, and it chooses recognisable hotels. It can write the copy, not decide who you are. Hotels will split in two: those an AI knows how to recommend, and those it sends back to Booking.

  5. The biggest risk isn't Booking.

    Booking does its job: it brings demand to those who don't generate it themselves. The real risk is a useful analysis left in a drawer, put off until after the season and then until after Christmas.
