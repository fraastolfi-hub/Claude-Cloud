N°01

The calculation that sums up twenty years of work.

It comes to four lines, and you probably know them already.

Line 1, location. Worth what the area is worth. It doesn't depend on you.

Line 2, square metres. Times the local value. It's arithmetic, not merit.

Line 3, the state of the building. This is where renovations go, already depreciated.

Line 4, EBITDA. Times a local multiple: the one for property, not for businesses.

The guests who have been coming back for ten years, the way your team works: they're on no line. The valuer values what can be transferred. An identity that lives only in your head can't be transferred.

01

The valuation counts the building, not the brand.

If you sell, the price is location plus square metres plus EBITDA. The brand is worth zero: there's nothing to transfer.

A hotel with an identity is valued like a business. Without one, like a building.

02

Your identity depends on one person.

It lives in your head: not in documents, not in the team, not on the website. If you're away for a month, your communication goes generic again.

For management, it's a limit. For ownership, it's a risk to the asset.

03

The aesthetic edge is temporary.

Your refurbishment is recent, but the hotel nearby is renovating now, with the same studio. And in the calculation, renovations are already depreciated.

Looks expire. Identity stays.

04

Investment goes where it shows.

Renovation, furniture, technology. Less often strategy, training and marketing.

Those are exactly the items that build the value the valuer can't see today.

05

Your guests come through the OTAs.

A large share of bookings goes through the portals, and those guests stay theirs.

A buyer won't pay for guests who belong to someone else.

06

Few searches by name.

Direct bookings aren't growing, despite a new website and campaigns. People find you as “hotel + area”.

Demand that doesn't carry your name can't be transferred.

07

Your reviews don't say why.

“Clean. Friendly. We'll be back.” What's missing is the one that says: “The only place where I found X.”

There's no written proof of what sets you apart.

08

The team changes every season.

People work for “a hotel like many others”. The service depends on who's there that year.

Identity serves the guest, but also the people who work with you.

09

Every decision reopens the debate.

New restaurant: which concept? New spa: which philosophy? Every time, you start from scratch.

With a defined identity, decisions follow from the positioning.

10

Occupancy known, margin per room less so.

Full occupancy is reassuring. You look at margin per room and per guest less often.

Whoever values your hotel looks at every number.

FAQ

The questions I'm asked most often.

Is the valuer wrong to ignore the brand?

No. A valuer values what can be transferred. The work is making the brand transferable: written down, applied by the team, visible in direct bookings.

What does “written identity” mean?

A positioning set down on a page, applied in the copy, the scripts and the decisions, and verifiable in your direct-booking numbers. As long as it lives in one person's head, it's worth nothing to anyone else.

To me, the hotel is an income property. Is this for me?

Probably not. If the brand isn't a priority, you need a property adviser, not positioning. Better to say so now.

I have several hotels. Can it be done?

Yes, and that's where it pays back most: each hotel set apart from the others in the group and from the market. You still start with one.
