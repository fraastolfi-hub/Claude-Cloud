The problem · Too much OTA commission

Rely less on the OTAs.

OTA commission takes a bigger bite out of your margin every year. For an 80-room hotel it comes to about €150,000 a year.

Booking.com brings bookings, but the guest stays theirs. The cause is rarely the channel: the guest can't find a reason to book with you.

N°01

Booking.com is a channel. It shouldn't be the channel.

The goal isn't to leave Booking. It's to know what it costs you, and to cut its share where you can.

Booking brings bookings. It doesn't bring you guests of your own.

The email address, the relationship and the next booking stay with the portal. You fill the rooms; Booking builds its brand.

Past half of your bookings, it's no longer a channel. It's a dependency.

It shrinks when guests search for your hotel by name. And they search for it when they know why to choose it.

01

The answer to “Why you?”

A guest asks: “What makes you different?” You answer: “Care, quality, attention.” They thank you and book another hotel on Booking.

If the answer doesn't fit in thirty seconds, it isn't clear yet.

02

No filter on enquiries.

You accept every booking. Then you notice that guests don't understand what you offer.

Being specific means choosing. And choosing means leaving some guests out.

03

Booking is your first channel.

It brings 60 bookings in every 100 and keeps 18%. With 80 rooms, that's about €150,000 a year.

Beyond a certain share, it's no longer a channel: it's a dependency.

04

Free upgrades in low season.

The room was empty, the upgrade costs nothing. But guests learn to wait for a deal, even in July.

A discount, repeated, teaches guests not to pay full price.

05

Rates unchanged for years.

You're afraid of losing bookings. A rise mostly loses you the guests least suited to your hotel.

Low rates attract guests who choose on price.

06

A website that lists services.

“4-star hotel. Spa. Restaurant. Relaxation.” With another logo, it would fit a hundred hotels.

Among a hundred similar hotels, guests pick the cheapest.

07

Campaigns that return little.

€2,000 of ads, 50 clicks, 2 bookings. Anyone clicks; the guests who book are the ones who understood what you offer.

Advertising amplifies the message. Even when it's generic.

08

Interchangeable reviews.

“Clean. Friendly. Good value for money.” What's missing is the one that says: “The only place where I found…”

Reviews reflect your positioning. If it's generic, so are they.

09

The seasonal seesaw.

Discounts and promotions in low season, full in high season. Then the cycle starts again.

You react to the season instead of building demand of your own. That's the ground where the OTAs win.

10

The guests lost after a rate rise.

You raise the rate and the guests who only look at price go elsewhere. It feels like a loss.

Often it's a filter: the room goes to the guests who see its value.

N°03

N°04

First the compass. Then the walls.

Case · Silva Splendid · Fiuggi

From “4-star hotel with spa” to The Wellness Hotel of Fiuggi.

  1. 118 rooms and the largest spa in Lazio. Yet it spoke to everyone, and nobody remembered it.
  2. The choice: a fact instead of an adjective, a category with the definite article (in Italian, L’Hotel Benessere di Fiuggi), and almost no families any more.
  3. In five years, revenue goes from €5.6 million to €7 million. The average rate roughly doubles.
Annual revenue €5.6M €7M

Positioning and renovation together: the first set the direction, the owners' investment made it credible. In that order.

For direct sales, the case is a different one. Veridia Resort, the nature resort of Chia, sells over 60% direct. I don't have a before-and-after revenue comparison, so I don't present one.

FAQ

The questions I'm asked most often.

Do I have to leave Booking?

No. Booking stays a channel, but stops being the main one. When guests search for your hotel by name, direct bookings grow. The work is giving them a reason to do it.

Do I need a new website?

No. You change the headline, the introduction and the key copy. A new site comes later, and only if needed.

Does it work for a smaller hotel too?

Yes, often better. A 40-room hotel can choose a precise niche; a 200-room hotel has to hold several segments together.

What do AI assistants have to do with commission?

Booking shows a hundred hotels. An AI assistant recommends two or three: the ones that are recognisable, specific and consistent on every channel. It can only describe well what is already clear.
