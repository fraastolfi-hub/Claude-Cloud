The problem · A hotel like all the others

Make your hotel recognisable.

Your guests love your hotel. They just can't say why. Often your website can't either.

Design, photos and a rebrand can be bought, and plenty of hotels have them. What you can't buy is a reason to choose you over another well-kept hotel.

The difference isn't in how your hotel looks. It's in the reason it exists, put in a way the guest recognises.

01

The covered-logo test.

Without the logo, your homepage copy fits a hundred hotels: “Elegance. Charm. An authentic experience.”

That's your competitors' vocabulary, not an identity.

02

Excellent reviews, but interchangeable.

“Wonderful property. Impeccable staff. We'll be back.” The same words appear in your competitors' reviews. What's missing: “The only place where I found X.”

If nobody writes it, X isn't perceived yet.

03

The aesthetic edge is temporary.

Your refurbishment is recent. The hotel nearby is renovating now, often with the same studio.

An aesthetic edge expires. An identity stays.

04

“Boutique hotel” no longer sets you apart.

Twenty years ago it was a position. Today it's a category, and a crowded one.

Saying “boutique hotel” today is almost the same as saying “hotel”.

05

You describe the destination, not your hotel.

The website talks about Sardinia, Tuscany, the lake.

That way, you promote every competitor in the area too.

06

Direct bookings aren't growing.

New website, new booking engine, campaigns running. But guests search for “boutique hotel + area”, not for your name.

And in that search, you're one of many.

07

The answer to “Why you?”

A guest asks: “What makes you different?” You answer: “Care, quality, attention.”

If the answer doesn't fit in thirty seconds, it isn't clear yet.

08

No filter on guests.

You accept every request. Then you notice that guests don't understand what you offer.

An offer for everyone is specific to no one.

09

Your identity depends on one person.

It lives in your head: not in documents, not in the team, not on the website. If you're away for a month, your communication goes generic again.

For management, it's a limit. For ownership, it's a risk to the asset.

10

Every decision reopens the debate.

New restaurant: which concept? New spa: which philosophy? Every time, you start from scratch.

With a defined identity, decisions follow from the positioning.

N°04

First the compass. Then the walls.

Case · Silva Splendid · Fiuggi

From “4-star hotel with spa” to The Wellness Hotel of Fiuggi.

  1. 118 rooms and the largest spa in Lazio. Yet it spoke to everyone, and nobody remembered it.
  2. The choice: a fact instead of an adjective, a category with the definite article (in Italian, L’Hotel Benessere di Fiuggi), and almost no families any more.
  3. In five years, revenue goes from €5.6 million to €7 million. The average rate roughly doubles.

Positioning and renovation together: the first set the direction, the owners' investment made it credible. In that order.

FAQ

The questions I'm asked most often.

I've already had a rebrand. Isn't that the same thing?

No. A rebrand decides how your hotel looks. Positioning decides why guests choose it. The first without the second is form without content.

What if my hotel is already positioned?

It may be. Try the test above: cover the logo. If the homepage fits only you, the position is there. If it fits a hundred hotels, it isn't. The positioning read checks this on your homepage, next to your competitors'.

Does it work for a group with several hotels?

Yes, and that's where it pays back most: each hotel set apart from the others in the group and from the market. You still start with one.

Do I need a new website?

Rarely from scratch. What it says needs revising: that costs less and matters more.
