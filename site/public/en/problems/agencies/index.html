Give your agency a clear brief.

It doesn't mean every agency was poor. Each one got the same brief, and did what that brief allowed.

N°01

If three chefs get the same dish wrong, check the recipe.

One chef who gets a dish wrong may not be good enough. If three get it wrong, the problem is the recipe.

In marketing, the recipe is called a brief. It often reads like this:

“Get us known. Showcase the property. Let's focus on quality.”

With these instructions, even an excellent agency writes charm, elegance, authentic experience. The same words it uses for the other hotels it works for.

Sometimes the agency really isn't up to the job. You can tell by one sign: it asks no questions.

If the questions came and the answers were missing, changing agency isn't enough.

01

The brief fits in three lines.

“Get us known. Showcase the property. Let's focus on quality.”

With these instructions, any agency produces the same thing.

02

The answer to “Why you?”

The agency asks: “What makes you different?” You answer: “Care, quality, attention.” And the agency writes exactly that.

If the answer isn't clear to you, it can't be clear to the agency.

03

No questions at the start.

The agency started from the content plan, not from your hotel's identity. And nobody noticed.

That one is the agency's limit. The others were perhaps waiting for answers.

04

Polished content, few bookings.

The profile is tidy and the content is good. The bookings come from other channels.

Polish doesn't replace a message.

05

Campaigns that return little.

€2,000 of ads, 50 clicks, 2 bookings. Anyone clicks; the guests who book are the ones who understood what you offer.

Advertising amplifies the message. Even when it's generic.

06

“It takes time.”

A six-month contract, then another six. Time passes, the result stays the same.

Time doesn't correct a vague instruction: it repeats it.

07

Every three years, back to square one.

New website, new agency, new campaign. Things improve for a few months, then go back to where they were.

You've been working on the last step for years.

08

A website that lists services.

“4-star hotel. Spa. Restaurant. Relaxation.” With another logo, it would fit a hundred hotels.

The agency wrote what it was given.

09

Well-written copy, but all the same.

Descriptions and posts generated with AI. Everyone writes better, everyone says the same things.

Writing well is the minimum today. It doesn't set you apart: it levels everyone out.

10

Every campaign starts from zero.

New message, new idea, new tone. Every time, opinions win.

With a defined identity, decisions follow from the positioning.

N°03

The agency's result is a sign. The cause is in the brief.

N°04

First the compass. Then the walls.

Case · Silva Splendid · Fiuggi

From “4-star hotel with spa” to The Wellness Hotel of Fiuggi.

  1. 118 rooms and the largest spa in Lazio. Yet it spoke to everyone, and nobody remembered it.
  2. The choice: a fact instead of an adjective, a category with the definite article (in Italian, L’Hotel Benessere di Fiuggi), and almost no families any more.
  3. In five years, revenue goes from €5.6 million to €7 million. The average rate roughly doubles.
Annual revenue €5.6M €7M

Positioning and renovation together: the first set the direction, the owners' investment made it credible. In that order.

FAQ

The questions I'm asked most often.

Should I change agency?

Not necessarily. You need to give it clear instructions. Positioning is the brief it needs, and many agencies work much better with that in hand.

How do I tell if the agency really isn't up to the job?

By one sign: it asks no questions. If it asks and the answers are missing, changing it won't help. You need the answers.

Who puts it into practice?

Your team or your suppliers, with a clear direction. I provide the positioning, the key copy and the scripts; they carry them out.

Do I need a new website?

Rarely from scratch. What it says needs revising: that costs less and matters more.
